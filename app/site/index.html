How it works

Three moves. No hand-written parsers, no rebuilt boilerplate.

01

Map your intake once

Describe how your questionnaire's answers fill a site's config in one declarative mapping document — validated with every problem reported at once. When your questionnaire changes, you edit the mapping, never a parser.

02

Bring or build templates

Start from the bundled catalog, upload templates built to the authoring rulebook, or chat one into existence in the Template Studio — generation is included, no model key to bring. Everything you add is private to your account.

03

Assemble, QA, deploy

One call parses a client's answers and assembles a real Next.js site. A QA bar — routes, links, console, accessibility with real contrast math, 375 px overflow — gates every build. Deploys go to hosting you own.

What you're standing on

Deterministic where it must be. Yours where it matters.

A deterministic core

Assembly is a deterministic engine — the same config always yields the same site. Nothing generative is ever required inside a build.

Your key is the license

No installers, no license files, no activation servers. Scoped API keys with transparent usage metering — that's the whole commercial machinery.

Private by account

Templates you upload, mappings you store, sites you assemble — visible to your account alone. Other licensees can't list them, read them, or collide with their names.

Bring your own everything

Sites deploy with your Vercel, GitHub, and database credentials — hosting you own, on your own tiers. Template generation is built in. Stardrive is infrastructure, not a middleman.

A QA bar that says no

Every build passes route rendering, link integrity, zero console errors, an axe accessibility scan with real contrast checks, and a 375 px overflow test — or it doesn't advance.

No hostage-taking

The assembled repo is your client's site and your code — exportable at any time. Client data is transient by default: job lifetime plus a grace window, then gone.

FAQ

The questions that matter.

Who is Stardrive for?

Agencies, studios, and freelancers who build marketing sites for clients and are tired of rebuilding the same machinery — intake parsing, template plumbing, QA, deployment — for every project. If you have a questionnaire and a design point of view, Stardrive is the rest.

Do my clients ever see Stardrive?

No. Stardrive is white-label infrastructure. Your clients see your brand, your process, and a finished site on their domain, deployed to hosting you or they own. The client relationship stays entirely yours.

What do I own?

Everything you bring and everything that comes out: your templates, your mappings, your intake data, and the assembled site repos. Sites deploy with your own Vercel, Turso, and GitHub credentials — connected in the Workbench, encrypted at rest, never displayed again — to accounts and plan tiers you choose. Stardrive never owns your website.

Do I get the engine?

You get its output — and that's the point. Assembled sites are standalone Next.js repos with zero Stardrive runtime dependency, deployable and editable anywhere, forever. The engine itself stays server-side: it is never included in an export, a deploy, or a repo push. Your clients can't see it, and neither can you — which is exactly why your exported sites can never break when Stardrive changes.

What does "built inside Deneb4" mean?

Stardrive is the productized engine of Deneb4, a working web studio. Every layer — the field-mapping engine, the template contracts, the QA bar — runs Deneb4's own client pipeline first. Nothing ships to licensees that isn't already earning its keep in production.

What's the current state?

Private beta. The API, the Workbench console, per-account privacy, the template pipeline, and the Studio are live today; the full assembly-and-deploy path is in staged rollout with the founding cohort. Honest status over promises — ask us anything at the form below.
